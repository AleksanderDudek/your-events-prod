Who ultimately decides who has the right to belong? Visibility does not always guarantee freedom, just as invisibility does not necessarily mean exclusion. Sometimes, the most visible groups claim the right to represent others and dictate the boundaries of acceptability. This workshop explores these core tensions through photography, personal associations, and an anonymous collective experiment. The session opens with a brief introduction to the art project Зноў | Znów | Again, which focuses on Belarusian communities in exile, their evolving identity, and the mechanisms of inclusion and exclusion. We will discuss photography, provocative posters, and spatial reactions in the urban landscape, examining how groups formed in the name of freedom and solidarity can sometimes unintentionally reproduce traditional hierarchies. Following the introduction, participants will take an independent walk through the How to See in the Dark exhibition. Guided by core questions about what is visible, invisible, included, or excluded, everyone will record their thoughts, concepts, and observations on anonymous cards. These cards will then be gathered, shuffled, and randomly redistributed—detaching each thought from its original author and context.
